What this project is about

AIXI is a formal idea: an agent that treats the world as a sequence of observations, actions, and rewards, maintains a prior over possible environments, and chooses actions that maximize expected future reward.

01

Universal learning

The agent does not begin with a narrow task-specific model. It starts with a broad hypothesis class and updates as evidence arrives.

02

Sequential decision making

Every action is a choice under uncertainty. The agent weighs tradeoffs across immediate reward and long-term outcomes.

03

Generality

The ambition is not just to perform one task well, but to adapt across many tasks with a common decision framework.

Core ideas

AIXI is less a product than a theoretical benchmark: a definition of what an ideal general agent would look like if it had to operate in a world it did not fully understand.

  • Model uncertainty explicitly instead of pretending the world is fully known.
  • Use simplicity as a prior to prefer parsimonious explanations of observations.
  • Act according to expected value over the full future horizon, not just the next step.
  • Keep the objective aligned with reward feedback while acknowledging limits of the model.

Why the theory matters

1

It asks the right question

What would an agent do if it had to learn from scratch while interacting with a complex environment?

2

It connects disciplines

It blends algorithmic information theory, decision theory, and reinforcement learning into one frame.

3

It identifies the hard part

Even if the theory is elegant, the computational burden is immense, which is why real systems remain approximations.
